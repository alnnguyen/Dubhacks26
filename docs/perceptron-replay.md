## Setup and use

1. Add `PERCEPTRON_API_KEY` to ignored `server/.env`. Optional `PERCEPTRON_MODEL` defaults to `perceptron-mk1.5`; see `server/.env.perceptron.example`. Never use a `VITE_` variable for the key.
2. Restart the updated game backend with `cd server && npm run dev` (or build and `npm start`). Those scripts load `server/.env`. The browser must connect to this updated backend; a key on your laptop does not configure a separately hosted shared server.
3. Run the frontend normally. Local Vite dev proxies `/replay` to the same `GAME_PROXY_TARGET` as Socket.IO. A separately deployed frontend uses its `VITE_GAME_SERVER_URL`; production hosting must route `/replay/*` to the updated backend.
4. During saber setup, everyone visible must agree to recording; enable the local replay checkbox. Once both fighters are armed, it records the first uninterrupted webcam segment, capped at 45 seconds, 640×360 at 15 fps, without audio. Losing fight readiness/camera stops the webcam segment. Local arena capture continues through the match, retaining motion from the opening 45 seconds plus a rolling 12-second closing window. **Stop replay recording**, discard or leaving stops capture. This is bounded capture, not an unlimited video recording.
5. Stay on the result screen. Preview/save/discard the local video. To analyze, confirm everyone visible agrees to send it to Perceptron, then choose **Create AI highlights**.
6. Before analysis, the cinematic arena player offers explicitly labeled server-event highlights. After analysis, **Play cinematic reel** uses Perceptron's selected intervals/captions for the earlier scenes. Automatic cameras alternate orbit, low and overhead views; the buttons override these viewpoints. Recorded joints, blades and board cells replay swings and sidesteps continuously. Impacts slow to 0.24× with a brief freeze, sparks, shock ring and camera kick; ordinary model moments without an impact play at 0.7×. A wipe separates scenes.
7. A captured server knockout always closes the reel. The director reserves its last 2.5 seconds, removes/shortens overlapping earlier selections, freezes the final pose, and labels it **Server-confirmed finale**. It checks the finished result, defeated player health and terminal winning hit; it does not promote a prior hit, forfeit or missing event into a knockout. The closing arena capture can supply this scene after webcam recording ends; that scene is not attributed to Perceptron.
8. The original webcam/model evidence player remains below and follows the arena playback when the corresponding video exists. Late arena-only moments show an explicit notice. Its original highlight controls still offer 0.65× cropped webcam playback. Reduce motion uses steady cameras, normal speed and no replay impact effects. No replay alters scores or sends combat commands.

Developer preview: `/?dev=replay` renders synthetic fighters, a sidestep, simulated clashes/hits/knockout and fixture captions. No camera, match, key or model call is used there; it is labeled throughout. This is presentation QA, not sponsor inference evidence.

## Local arena recording and synchronization

- The existing `arena`, `saberResolved`, `projectileLanded` and `view` subscriptions are read-only. The webcam recorder reports its actual `performance.now()` start to the motion collector; frame/event arrival times are converted to seconds from that epoch. No browser wall-clock/server clock comparison is assumed.
- Motion capture starts only when opt-in video recording actually starts. The opening 45 seconds and closing 12 seconds are retained, at most 1900 deep-copied arena frames and 240 outcomes. No full-match pose history is uploaded. Discard clears the arrays and stops recording.
- The server's final frame may follow its finished view. Capture accepts up to 200 ms of these closing frames so the terminal pose can be shown. The director interpolates recorded frames within short intervals and rejects gaps rather than inventing motion across the omitted middle of a long match.
- Impacts come from server messages, not model captions. Sparks are positioned from the recorded defender joints/blades, which approximates an effect anchor rather than recovering the exact collision contact. Final hit matching requires the last winning hit within 200 ms before the finished view and the defeated slot at zero health.
- If a projectile causes the knockout, its terminal outcome takes precedence over a nearby earlier saber hit and is captioned as a finishing shot/throw. The saber arena frames do not contain projectile flight geometry, so a bolt/rock trajectory is not reconstructed.
- Webcam/arena alignment is approximate: messages arrive after network delay and the live display has its own smoothing. Both fighters' joints are still client-reported under the game's existing trust model. This is not anti-cheat evidence, a physical reconstruction, or guaranteed frame-accurate score/video synchronization.

## Interfaces and limits

- Additive `GameClient.analyzeReplay()` requests `replay:ticket({matchId})` over the joined socket, then uploads a raw Blob to `POST /replay/:matchId`. No private player ID in HTTP, no permanent key in browser responses.
- The current finished match and connected pilot are checked at ticket issuance and upload. Tickets expire in two minutes. Existing demo sessions remain trust-based, as documented in contracts; this does not introduce production account authentication.
- Accepted transport MIME types: WebM or MP4, maximum 8 MiB, declared duration ≤45 seconds. The server bounds the upload and reads it in memory, with a 15-second body deadline. Encoded duration is client-reported rather than independently decoded; provider rejection is explicit.
- Provider endpoint: `https://api.perceptron.inc/v1/chat/completions`, using `video_url` with inline data, `vision_config.annotation_format: clip` then `box`. At most two calls per analysis, with a shared 45-second inference deadline and no automatic retries. Native `<clip>` and `<track>/<point_box>` annotations are parsed, bounded, and normalized.
- Two concurrent uploads/analyses globally, eight analysis attempts per five minutes. Bounded cache: 64 pilot/match jobs with a ten-minute TTL. Concurrent/repeated uploads for the same pilot/match share the original job, including failures, so a retry does not incur new inference until expiry. Leaving the match invalidates access even to cached results.
- Recordings and provider payloads are never saved on the game server's disk. Video is sent inline to Perceptron only after consent; provider retention follows their policies. The game keeps only parsed analysis promises/results in its temporary in-memory cache.
- Captions are rendered as plain React text. Missing framing keeps the original view and is labeled. Missing/invalid moments, a missing key, unsupported browser recording, oversize uploads, provider failure and offline state are explicit; there are no fabricated fallback highlights.

## Scope boundaries

This version creates an in-browser cinematic presentation from recorded game geometry, with an original-webcam-video download. The orbit/low/overhead cameras are new views of that recorded virtual arena, not new views reconstructed from the webcam. It does not export a rendered highlight movie, provide free mouse-drag camera control, or draw a Perceptron saber-tip trail. Player boxes are webcam framing, not saber tracking; model captions do not prove scored contact. Real-duel quality inherits the original pose data. The full consenting-duel/provider-to-3D flow and physical-device performance still require acceptance testing.

Provider references checked: [official quickstart](https://docs.perceptron.inc/perceptron-mk1.5/index), [official Python SDK](https://github.com/perceptron-ai-inc/perceptron), [native annotation grammar](https://github.com/perceptron-ai-inc/perceptron/blob/main/src/perceptron/pointing/parser.py).
